A first conversation about a mortgage is often stressful: lots of new terms, numbers and documents. The good news is that you do not need to understand banking. You just need to come prepared and know what to ask.

## 1. Define your goal

Before you talk to a bank or a mortgage adviser, answer a few questions:

- What do you want to buy: a new-build flat, a resale flat or a house?
- In which area, and in what price range?
- When do you want to buy: within a month, six months, a year?
- Are you buying alone or with someone else?

These answers make it quicker to see which options are realistic.

## 2. Work out your deposit

Banks usually expect a deposit. The minimum depends on the bank and on any scheme you use. Check how much of your savings can go towards the deposit and how much you need to keep for other costs: notary fees, taxes, commissions, moving and furnishing.

## 3. Gather basic information about your income

You usually do not need a full set of documents for the first conversation. It does help to know:

- what kind of contract you work on (employment contract, self-employment, civil-law contract),
- how long you have had your current source of income,
- your regular commitments: other loans, credit cards, overdrafts, leases.

Credit card and overdraft limits can reduce how much you can borrow, even if you never use them.

## 4. If you are not a Polish citizen

Banks may have extra requirements for foreigners, for example about your residence status, how long you have worked in Poland or the currency of your income. These differ between banks, so ask about them at the very start.

## 5. Prepare a list of questions

These questions help you compare offers:

1. What is the **RRSO** (the Polish equivalent of APR) and what does it include?
2. What is the margin and can it change?
3. Is the rate fixed or variable, and for how long?
4. Which extra products are required (insurance, an account, a card)?
5. What does early repayment cost?
6. How long does the process take and which documents will be needed later?

> Do not hesitate to ask for explanations. A good mortgage conversation is one after which you understand what you are signing.

## 6. Do not decide on the spot

The first conversation is for gathering information. Give yourself time to compare offers and think about the terms. The bank makes the lending decision anyway, after reviewing your application and documents.

## Summary

Before the meeting: define your goal, work out your deposit, gather information about your income and prepare your questions. If conversations in Polish are hard for you, or you would like to prepare together, we can do that.
