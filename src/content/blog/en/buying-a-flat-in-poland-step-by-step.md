Buying a flat in Poland happens in several stages. The order can vary, especially between new-build (from a developer) and resale, but the general pattern is similar.

## 1. Budget and financing

Before you start viewing flats, it helps to know what you can afford. If you plan to take out a mortgage, an early conversation with a bank or mortgage adviser will help you set a realistic budget.

Remember the extra costs: notary and court fees, possible taxes, any agent’s commission and furnishing.

## 2. Searching and viewings

At viewings, look beyond the flat itself to the building, the area, running costs and legal status. Prepare questions for the seller, for example:

- Who owns it, and does the property have a land and mortgage register entry (księga wieczysta)?
- Is the flat subject to a mortgage or other rights?
- What are the monthly fees to the owners’ association (wspólnota) or housing cooperative?
- When could the flat be handed over?

## 3. Checking the property

You can view the land and mortgage register online if you know its number. Assessing the legal status and documents, however, is a job for a specialist such as a lawyer or notary. It is worth doing, especially the first time you buy.

## 4. Reservation and preliminary contract

Once you have chosen a flat, the parties often sign a preliminary contract (umowa przedwstępna). It sets out, among other things, the price, the date of the final contract and the terms of the deposit, which may be a zadatek or a zaliczka. These two are different and work differently if the deal falls through. Ask a lawyer or notary to explain the difference before you sign.

## 5. The mortgage

If you are buying with a mortgage, you usually apply to the bank after signing the preliminary contract. The bank reviews your situation and the property, then makes its decision. Only after a positive decision do you sign the loan agreement.

## 6. The notarial deed

Transferring ownership of property requires a notarial deed. The notary reads the deed, explains its content and charges the fees. If you do not speak Polish well, the notary may require a sworn translator to be present. Ask about this in advance.

## 7. Handover

Finally, the flat is handed over, usually with a handover report. Note down the meter readings and the keys you receive.

## Summary

Each stage has its own questions and documents. I help you understand what is happening at each step, prepare your questions and coordinate appointments. Leave the legal review of documents to a properly qualified specialist.
