In the property market you will meet plenty of people who "help with the transaction". It is worth knowing **whose interests** each of them serves. That makes conversations easier and prevents misunderstandings about commission.

## The seller’s agent

When the seller signs a contract with an estate agency, that agency’s agent works for the seller. Their job is to sell the property, ideally quickly and at a good price. They may be friendly and helpful to the buyer, but they represent the other side.

## The buyer’s intermediary

A buyer can sign their own contract with an intermediary who searches for property on their behalf and protects their interests. That is a separate contract with its own terms and fee.

## Who pays the commission?

It depends on the contracts. You will see different models:

- only the seller pays,
- only the buyer pays,
- both pay, each to their own intermediary or to the same agency.

So always ask directly: **who pays the commission, how much and when?** The answer should be written into the contract.

## Conflict of interest

If the same person or agency acts for both seller and buyer, their interests may conflict, for example on price. This is not necessarily a problem, but it should be clearly disclosed.

## What to check before signing with an intermediary

1. On whose behalf does the intermediary act?
2. What exactly does the service include?
3. What is the fee and when is it due?
4. Do they also charge the other party?
5. Is the contract exclusive, and for how long?
6. Do they have professional liability insurance?

> Ask for the contract in writing and read it before you sign. If something is unclear, ask, and if in doubt, consult a lawyer.
